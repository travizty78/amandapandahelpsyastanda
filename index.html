A Standing & Balance Companion

Helping babies find their footing, one encouraging word at a time.

Amanda Panda Helps Ya' Standa is a plush companion parents place just out of reach — so babies climb, pull up, and stand, and hear her cheer them on the moment they get there.

See How She Works For Parents & Clinicians
Standing is one of the biggest milestones of a baby's first year — and one of the hardest to practice alone. She gives babies a reason to reach, climb, and try again.

How She Works

Three simple steps, every time.

No screens, no setup app, no batteries to charge mid-nap. Just a plush bear and a real reason to stand.

1

Place her up high

A parent sets her on the arm of a couch or the crib rail, just above where the baby is playing, and presses her chest button to say hello.

2

Baby reaches & stands

Drawn by her voice, the baby crawls over, pulls up on the furniture, and grabs hold of her velcro paws to reach a full stand.

3

She cheers them on

A squeeze presses her button again — babies quickly learn they can trigger her encouragement themselves, and do it again, and again.

Press The Button

Eight real, recorded phrases.

For Every Kind of Learner

Built for the milestone, not just the moment.

Every baby reaches standing on their own timeline. For some, that timeline needs a little more repetition, a little more sensory motivation, or a little more encouragement than words alone provide — and that's true for typically developing babies and for babies with autism or delayed milestones alike.

She was designed around consistent, predictable, positive reinforcement: the same warm voice, the same eight phrases, the same gentle velcro grip, every single time. Parents and occupational therapists can use her as a repeatable practice tool, not a one-time novelty.

🔊

Consistent auditory cue

The same eight phrases, every time — predictability that supports repetition-based learning.

🖐️

Tactile engagement

Soft plush and velcro paws give babies something secure to grip while pulling to stand.

💛

Positive-only reinforcement

Every phrase is encouragement — there's no wrong way to reach for her.

Our Story
